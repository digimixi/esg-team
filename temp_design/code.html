What are Graphite Electrodes? (什麼是石墨電極？)

Graphite electrodes are large-scale conducting components primarily composed of high-quality petroleum or needle coke and coal tar pitch. In an Electric Arc Furnace (EAF), they play a critical role as the primary conduit for electricity to reach the furnace charge. As current passes through these electrodes, an electric arc is generated between the electrode tips and the raw material, producing the intense thermal energy required for melting and refining steel.

ELECTRICAL CONDUCTION

High-purity graphite offers low electrical resistance, ensuring efficient power transfer with minimal heat loss in the electrode itself.

THERMAL STABILITY

Capable of withstanding temperatures up to 3,500°C without significant structural degradation or mechanical failure.

Common Questions (常見問題)

How often do graphite electrodes need to be replaced? expand_more
Electrode consumption varies by furnace efficiency and power grade. On average, consumption rates range from 1.0kg to 2.5kg per ton of liquid steel produced. UHP grades typically offer the lowest consumption rates due to higher resistance to oxidation.
What factors influence the price of graphite electrodes? expand_more
Pricing is primarily driven by the cost of Needle Coke (the key raw material), environmental regulations impacting production in China and Europe, and global demand for recycled steel. Market volatility is managed through long-term supply agreements.
Can they be used outside of steel manufacturing? expand_more
Yes, they are also utilized in the production of yellow phosphorus, silicon metal, and ferroalloys. Any industrial process requiring high-temperature arc melting can utilize specialized graphite electrodes.
